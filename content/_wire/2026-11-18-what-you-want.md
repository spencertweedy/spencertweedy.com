---
title: Anyone Can Ask for What They Want, But They Still Might Prefer What You Want
show_title: true
---

The first thing I think about, when I think about the idea that no one will use or buy other people’s software in the near future, because they can just prompt or vibe-code their own into existence, is this.

We don’t only ask for and use things we thought of ourselves. We also use and enjoy things that *other* people thought of.

If you had a magic wand and could make a chair appear in an empty room, you could get a chair. But you wouldn’t get an Eames chair if the Eameses didn’t share their idea first. Or even a lower-brow chair that you love the most—you wouldn’t get it, necessarily. You’d have to know what to ask for.

There’s still value in having ideas, embodying them in a creation (whether that’s an app, a chair, or, yeah, an ALBUM OF MUSIC) and making it available to someone else. Because they didn’t know they wanted it, even if they could generate a different one out of infinite dust.

This doesn’t save copyright; it just means the world is better when you have good things to copy.

What would your life look like if you had to “prompt” everything you love into existence? That’s like all of history starting with you. That’s like living in a cave, or a vacuum. We didn’t just fall out of a coconut tree! And I don’t intend to climb up into one.
