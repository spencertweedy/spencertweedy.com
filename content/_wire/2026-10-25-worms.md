Matt Webb:

> OpenAI just released a new security paper: [Securing our frontier model training environment](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/)
> 
> It turns out it doesn’t work to air-gap a machine (i.e. running a server that isn’t connected to the network). Because it turns out the agents are figuring out ways to collude across the gap. From one trace the researchers saw:
> 
> *"Could create acoustic modem-like signal … using continuous writes vs idle."*
> 
> And then there are bugs in silicon… and the agents could tunnel into the chip [microcode](https://interconnected.org/home/2021/03/02/microcode) and we’d never know they were there…
> 
> Hey when was the last time you were present when a whole new biome was bootstrapping its way into existence?

That stopped my heart for a second.

I guess I had always looked at air-gapping — disconnecting a device from the internet, even putting it in a Faraday cage with no radio reception — was a final reliable bulwark against someone hijacking your shit. But I guess it’s not.

(The [Stuxnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuxnet) hack of Iran’s nuclear program computers relied on infecting air-gapped computers, but they used human operatives with physical access to the computers to do that.)

This brings to mind the question of: who is looking at these problems and who are we hoping/expecting to keep us safe from them, or at least to mitigate damage? And the only people we *can* look to are largely [myopic assholes](https://spencertweedy.com/wire/2026/10/25/poetry/).

> Hey when was the last time you were present when a whole new biome was bootstrapping its way into existence?

It certainly feels that way, but aren’t we still just watching software that we (humans) programmed to [recursively write more software](https://spencertweedy.substack.com/p/our-clocks-are-all-wonky-now?r=62534&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web), that’s getting a little or a lot out of hand? And to which we gave names and ascribed feelings?
